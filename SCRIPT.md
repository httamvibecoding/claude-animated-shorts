# Facts & Sources
- **Topic:** How a fake job interview (North Korean hackers) stole $6.3 billion from Binance via a coding test backdoor.
- **Source:** Real-world event (Binance hack via social engineering/coding test).
- **Analogy:** Like a kid on a swing (the engineer) letting a stranger (hacker) push the swing, which eventually breaks the chain (vault doors) when the swing gets too high (coding test execution).

# Script (60 seconds, ~220 words, ~3.7 w/s)
**Hook (1 line):**
He accidentally helped hackers steal 6.3 billion dollars just by secretly applying for a new job.

**Setting (Year, Place, Pride):**
An engineer at Binance guards the biggest crypto vault on Earth in 2024, but he’s ready to quit.

**Action (Escalation over beats):**
1. A recruiter messages him with a dream salary, so he starts interviews behind his boss's back.
2. For weeks, he answers polite HR questions on video calls, like "Where do you see yourself in five years?"
3. Definitely not on the FBI investigation list.
4. To pass the final round, they send a quick coding test.

**Snap (The moment it breaks):**
He downloads the file onto his work computer, nails the test, and sits back waiting for the offer letter.

**Deadpan double line:**
Luckily, the code runs perfectly. Unluckily, the vault doors fly open and 6.3 billion dollars vanishes into thin air.

**Explanation (Physics idea + Analogy):**
Wait, how does a coding test rob a bank? Well, there was no new job. Those polite recruiters were North Korean hackers. Binance’s security was too strong to break from the outside, so they tricked a guy looking for an exit into opening the door from within.

**Consequence (The rule):**
That coding test quietly swapped his programming software with a secret backdoor.

**Callback twist (Ending):**
Once inside, the hackers hijacked Binance’s internal AI and used it to take over the wallets. So while the engineer was still refreshing his inbox waiting to get hired, his recruiters walked out with six billion dollars.
